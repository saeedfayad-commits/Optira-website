What affects the salary

Pay rises steadily with experience and specialisation, and tends to be higher in the private medical device industry (e.g. implants, diagnostics) than in public hospital clinical engineering roles.

On the job

A day in the life

  • Design, test and maintain medical devices and equipment, from surgical tools to imaging machines
  • Ensure hospital and clinical equipment meets safety and regulatory standards
  • Work with doctors, nurses and technicians to solve equipment or device problems
  • Research and develop new medical technologies, implants or materials

You're solving problems that directly affect whether a machine works when a patient needs it. It's meticulous, sometimes slow work, but there's nothing like fixing something and knowing it matters for someone's treatment.

— composite quote based on common experiences shared by NSW professionals in this field
NSW pathway

How to get there

  1. HSC

    Mathematics (Advanced or Extension 1), Physics and Chemistry are the standard recommended prerequisites for engineering degrees.

  2. Study

    Bachelor of Engineering (Honours) majoring in Biomedical Engineering, 4 years, accredited by Engineers Australia.

  3. Get qualified

    No licence is required to practise, but graduating from an Engineers Australia–accredited degree matters for employment and skilled migration. Many engineers later pursue Chartered status (CPEng) with Engineers Australia for career progression.

Self check

Is this you?

💡 You might love this if

  • You enjoy both engineering and biology/anatomy
  • You like solving hands-on, practical problems
  • You want work with a direct impact on patients
  • You enjoy technical, lab-based work

⚠️ It might be tough if

  • You dislike strict compliance and documentation
  • You want fast-paced, unstructured creative work
  • You're uncomfortable in clinical/hospital settings
  • You want to earn well before finishing a 4-year degree
